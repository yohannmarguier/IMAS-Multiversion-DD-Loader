# Silence is earned by mechanism coverage, not by the DD version pair

The shim converts DD paths and values underneath a caller that never asked, and returns `code == 0`. IMAS-Python — the reference HLI, written by the people who own the DD — refuses to do that across the DD 3 → DD 4 boundary. This ADR settles why the shim's behaviour is nonetheless defensible, and what the defence costs us. Much of the read-path design is downstream of it.

## What IMAS-Python actually does

The premise this decision started from was wrong, and the correction matters. IMAS-Python does **not** confine conversion to an explicit `convert_ids` call. `DBEntry.get` and `get_slice` take `autoconvert: bool = True` — conversion on read is the **default**, and it is silent. `docs/source/multi-dd.rst` *recommends* it for version pairs inside one major version, under a caution that reads: "The automatic conversion doesn't provide feedback when data cannot be converted between two versions of the data dictionary. Any incompatibilities between versions are silently ignored."

The refusal is narrower than the premise claimed. It fires only at the major-version boundary, on a literal comparison of the leading digit in `imas/db_entry.py`, and only when a conversion would actually be required — `autoconvert=False` builds the destination in the stored DD version, so no boundary is crossed and no error is raised. `put` refuses symmetrically. It arrived in one commit, `8f1db5f` (2025-11-13, Maarten Sebregts, "Disable implicit conversion between major versions of the DD"), whose message is the whole rationale:

> Implicit conversion between major versions of the DD is almost always giving incorrect results, so it's better to disallow this instead of emitting warnings (which people usually don't read).

Two clauses, both load-bearing here.

**The refusal is mechanism-specific.** IMAS-Python has two conversion mechanisms, not one. Automatic conversion applies the DD's non-backwards-compatible-change metadata — a rename map, and nothing else. Explicit `convert_ids` applies hand-written rules as well. The supported-conversions tables in `multi-dd.rst` measure the gap exactly: of the eight data conversions listed for DD 3 ↔ DD 4, automatic conversion supports **one**. Changed COCOS definition, the equilibrium contour tree, the `pulse_schedule` time-base restructuring, GGD `space/coordinates_type`, `pf_active` `circuit/connection`, open/closed contours and `ids_properties/source` → `provenance` are all "Explicit: Yes, Automatic: No". Every type change is likewise explicit-only, and where automatic conversion meets one, the destination quantity "will remain empty".

So "almost always giving incorrect results" is not a claim about silence. It is a claim about a rename-only mechanism meeting a boundary whose changes are mostly value and structural transformations. The shim's mechanism is not that mechanism: a path-level rule carries a value transformation and a fidelity verdict, so COCOS sign flips — the single largest item on that list — are expressed rather than ignored.

**A notification channel was tried and rejected.** Before that commit, the same situation emitted warnings. It was changed to a hard error *because the warnings did not protect anyone*. This is a direct adverse precedent for reporting loss through `al_status_t`: our channel is one `int` plus a 256-byte message, which is not obviously better read than a Python logger. Any defence of the shim that rests on "the caller will be told" is betting against a measured result.

## Decision

1. **Silence is earned by mechanism coverage, not by the DD version pair.** The shim converts where a path-level rule expresses the change, and does not convert where none does. We converge with IMAS-Python's reasoning rather than contradicting it: their line is drawn where their mechanism runs out, and ours is drawn in the same place — a different place, because it is a different mechanism.

2. **The DD 3 → DD 4 boundary gets no special treatment.** One policy across all 35 DD versions in the chain. Splitting on the major digit would reinstate the version-pair test that decision 1 rejects; IMAS-Python's major-digit comparison is a mechanism statement in a version costume, and copying the costume without the mechanism buys nothing.

3. **The floor is autoconvert-equivalence, and the shim already exceeds it.** Best-effort path conversion plus some retyping is the goal. Complex conversions — time-base interpolation, the contour tree, structural rebuilds — are **deliberately out of scope**, so `convert_ids` is a ceiling the shim consciously stops below, not a specification it fails to meet. A conversion-map artifact that does not cover them is correct, not defective.

4. **A DD path that no rule claims a source for returns not-found**, which on this ABI is `code == 0` with a null data pointer — IMAS-Core's own answer for an unfilled field, so no HLI changes and no HLI is surprised. This is superficially the same silence that sank `autoconvert`, and the difference is the whole point: `autoconvert` stayed silent on paths it *should have filled*, because its mechanism could not express the change. The shim stays silent only where the artifact asserts there is nothing to fill from.

5. **The HLI DD version is the conversion opt-in**, by either route settled in ADR 0005 — the setter or `IMAS_MVDD_HLI_DD_VERSION`. Unset means pure passthrough with no version discovery at all (ADR 0005), so nothing converts unless someone armed the shim. **This opt-in is deployment-time, not call-time**, and that limit is stated rather than glossed: the person who reads the data is not always the person who consented to conversion. IMAS-Python's opt-out is per `get`, chosen by whoever writes the analysis code. A call-time opt-in is structurally unavailable to us — the shim mirrors IMAS-Core's ABI and there is no argument to carry one — so this is a real divergence from the reference HLI, not an equivalent arrangement.

6. **A fidelity verdict says how lossy a rule is; it does not say whether the rule was checked.** These are two properties and only one exists. A conversion-map artifact must carry reviewedness — whether a rule's correctness is derivable from the DD or rests on a human judgement — and a rule resting on an unreviewed judgement must not convert silently. **The marker and its validation belong to the conversion-map generator effort**, which owns rule authoring; this ADR states the requirement and hands it over. Nothing is added to the artifact format, ADR 0004 is not amended, and no shim behaviour is defined here.

## Considered Options

- **Refuse across DD 3 → DD 4, as IMAS-Python does** — rejected. It is the safest position and it dissolves the effort: the validation scope is equilibrium 3.39.0 ⇄ 4.1.1, which *is* a major-boundary pair, so adopting the line would leave the shim with nothing it is allowed to convert and would require redrawing the destination.
- **Silence is fine because the loss channel makes degradation visible** — rejected on the evidence above. Commit `8f1db5f` ran that experiment with a richer notification channel than ours and abandoned it. The shim's defence must rest on mechanism, not on notification, and does.
- **Split on the fidelity verdict: exact converts silently, lossy refuses** — rejected as the *primary* test. It is version-agnostic and appealing, but `split-psi-axis` in the equilibrium artifact claims `forward="exact"` on an assumption its own note says may be wrong. A verdict-based gate would wave through exactly the rule most in need of review, which is why decision 6 separates reviewedness from fidelity instead of overloading the verdict.
- **Make the artifact's existing `decision="yes"` attribute machine-meaningful and refuse on it** — rejected as out of scope and as building on sand. The attribute is real but marginal: two rules carry it, a header comment defines it, `validate.py` — which that comment tells the reader to run — does not exist in this repository, no ADR mentions it, and no code reads it. Giving it runtime meaning would invent a feature and pull rule authoring into a map that ruled it a separate effort. Decision 6 states the requirement instead.
- **Treat IMAS-Python's explicit-conversion rule set as a reference the artifact must not contradict** — rejected, and this was the closest call. It contradicts the artifact today on roughly 42 equilibrium paths: `new-contour-tree` is `rel="right_only"` — no DD 3 source exists — while `convert_ids` fills `contour_tree` from `boundary_separatrix` and `boundary_secondary_separatrix`, the two structures the artifact drops as `left_only` (~30 paths and 12 paths respectively). That is a shipped counter-example, not a difference of physical opinion. It is nonetheless *not* a defect, because decision 3 puts that class of conversion out of scope by choice. `convert_ids` is prior art and a ceiling; it does not govern the artifact.
- **A separate conversion switch, independent of the HLI DD version** — rejected. It makes consent explicit at the cost of a second shim-owned export, a second latch, and a state where the stored DD version is known but conversion is off, for which no caller has asked.

## Consequences

- **The recorded disagreement with the reference HLI is narrow and stated.** The shim converts silently across the major boundary where IMAS-Python refuses. The argument is that IMAS-Python's refusal governs a rename-only mechanism which the shim exceeds, and that the shim declines what it cannot express instead of emptying it. A reviewer who finds IMAS-Python's `RuntimeError` and asks the obvious question should be sent here.
- **The consent gap is the weakest part of this position, and it is not closeable on this ABI.** Deployment-time opt-in is what the mirrored ABI permits. If that is judged insufficient, the remedy is not a shim change but a different integration story.
- **Autoconvert-equivalence is now a claim that something must prove.** It is asserted here from the supported-conversions tables, not measured. Proving the floor belongs to the validation gate.
- **Refusals already in force are unaffected and are what keeps decision 4 honest.** Unit redefinitions refuse (ADR 0010) and rank-changing retypes fail at the seam (ADR 0006). Decision 4 governs only paths no rule claims a source for; it does not soften a refusal into an empty read.
- **ADR 0007 gains independent corroboration.** IMAS-Python treats an IDS with no DD-version stamp exactly as the shim decided to: it warns and loads the data as though it were stored in the reader's own DD version. The shim's presumption that an unstamped IDS occurrence matches the HLI DD version is the reference HLI's behaviour too.
